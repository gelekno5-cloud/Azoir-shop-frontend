What to put on a signet ring, and how to make it yours.

Initials, a crest or a symbol of your own: how to choose the mark, how it is cut into the metal, and what to settle before you begin.

Published November 2026 · Azoir & Co., New York

The short answer

Put something on it that is genuinely yours and that still reads at the size of a fingernail. Initials or a monogram are the classic choice. A single personal symbol is the modern one. A coat of arms is right only if you have a real claim to it.

Whatever you choose, simpler marks wear better. A signet face is usually smaller than a postage stamp, and fine detail softens with years of daily wear.

Why symbols are back on signets

For most of its history a signet was a working tool: its owner pressed it into warm wax to seal a letter or a document. Today it is worn for meaning rather than for paperwork, and that has opened the face to more than heraldry.

You can see the shift in new collections. In November 2026, JCK reported that the jewelry brand Kinn built a new collection of 14k gold pendants and signet rings around four symbols, the butterfly, swan, Pegasus and crescent moon, and that the butterfly struck a particularly strong chord with customers (JCK). A symbol says something about the wearer without spelling out a name.

Choosing a symbol that lasts

The best symbols come from your own life, not from a list of meanings. A few starting points people often use:

  • Sky: a crescent moon or a star, for a birth, a night or a name.
  • Nature: a leaf, a flower or a tree, often one tied to a place or to the person the ring honors.
  • Animals: a bird, a lion, a horse or a butterfly, chosen for a trait or a story.
  • Objects: an anchor, a key or a compass, for a career, a home or a journey.

Two tests help. First, would you still choose it in twenty years? Second, does it work at 12 millimeters wide? Sketch it the size of your little fingernail. If it turns into a blur, simplify it before it reaches the metal.

A word on family crests

Many people want the crest they have seen printed beside their surname. Be careful. In the heraldic traditions of England and Scotland, a coat of arms belongs to an individual rather than a family, passing from one holder to the next, and in England grants of arms are controlled by the College of Arms (Wikipedia). Sharing a surname does not make the arms yours.

If a relative's signet already carries real arms, that is a lovely thing to continue, and our guide to inherited jewelry covers reworking a family piece. If not, design an emblem of your own. It will mean more than one borrowed from a website.

How the mark is cut

Surface engraving cuts fine lines into the face so the design reads the right way round on the ring. It suits monograms and symbols and is the most common choice. Seal engraving is cut deep and in mirror image so it leaves a clean impression in wax. It looks backwards on the hand, and it rewards a thicker face. Relief leaves the design raised above a cut-away ground, which gives a strong shadow but wears faster on its high points.

The face shape matters as much as the mark. An oval suits most hands and most monograms. A cushion or rectangle gives letters more room. A round face suits a single symbol. Ask to see the design at true size in a 3D render before anything is cast.

Metal, weight and price

A signet carries more metal than most rings, because the face is solid. That makes the gold price matter. On November 9, 2026, spot gold was reported at $4,111.44 an ounce (ChainCatcher), so every gram counts. A face hollowed from the back, a slimmer shank or 14K instead of 18K can keep the weight down without changing how the ring looks from the front.

For daily wear, 14K gold is the durable all-rounder, 18K gives a richer yellow, and platinum is the heaviest and naturally white. Our materials guide compares them, and care & maintenance covers keeping an engraved face crisp. A wide face can feel snugger than a thin band, so measure with the ring size guide on the finger you plan to use.

Questions

Good to know.

What should I put on a signet ring?

Something that is yours and that still reads at the size of a fingernail. Initials or a monogram are the classic choice. A single personal symbol, such as a crescent moon, a flower or an animal, is the modern one. A coat of arms suits you if you have a real right to it.

Can I use my family crest on a signet ring?

Only if it is actually yours. In English and Scottish heraldry, arms belong to one person and pass down a family line, not to everyone with the same surname. If a relative has a real grant, ask who holds it. If not, a personal emblem or a monogram is the honest alternative.

Which finger do you wear a signet ring on?

Traditionally the little finger, usually on the non-dominant hand. Today any finger is fine. Wide faces sit most comfortably on the little or index finger, so measure the finger you plan to use.

Why is the engraving backwards on some signet rings?

Because it was made to be pressed into wax. A seal-engraved signet is cut deep and in mirror image, so the impression it leaves reads the right way round. If you will never seal anything, a surface engraving that reads correctly on the ring is the simpler choice.
