Lab-grown or natural? An honest answer.

They are the same stone with two different origin stories. Here is what actually differs — price, value over time, how they're graded — and how to choose without being sold to.

Published November 2026 · Azoir & Co., New York

The same stone, twice

A lab-grown diamond is not an imitation, a simulant or "fake". It is carbon crystallized in the same structure as a mined diamond — chemically, physically and optically identical, with the same hardness, the same fire, the same everything. Cubic zirconia and moissanite are different materials; a lab-grown diamond is a diamond. The major gem laboratories grade both origins on the same four Cs — cut, color, clarity and carat.

The one real difference is the origin story: one stone formed over a billion years in the earth, the other grew in a matter of weeks in a machine. How much that story matters is a personal question, not a technical one — and it's the right question to sit with.

How a diamond is grown

Two methods dominate. HPHT (high pressure, high temperature) recreates the conditions of the deep earth around a diamond seed. CVD (chemical vapor deposition) grows the crystal layer by layer from carbon-rich gas. Both produce genuine diamond; both routinely achieve colors and clarities that are rare in nature. Whatever the method, the stone is then cut and polished by the same hands and tools as a mined one.

What the price difference buys

Lab-grown stones cost noticeably less than natural stones of the same size and quality — often less than half, and the gap has widened as growing technology matures. In a custom ring, that difference is a design decision: the same budget can move into a larger or finer stone, a heavier gold band, more intricate setting work — or simply stay in your pocket.

It cuts the other way too: if the center stone is lab-grown, there is less reason to compromise on the cut, and cut is what makes a diamond actually sparkle. Our materials guide covers how we balance the four Cs against a budget.

The value question, honestly

This is where most guides go quiet, so let's not. No diamond should be bought as an investment. Natural diamonds resell below what you paid at retail; lab-grown resale prices are lower still and less predictable, because the cost of growing them keeps falling. If resale value genuinely matters to you, that is a real argument for a natural stone — just weigh it against the certainty that you're paying more today for a value that is still speculative tomorrow.

What holds value in either case is the piece itself: a well-designed, well-made ring is worn for a lifetime and passed down. That's the return this purchase is actually for.

Can anyone tell?

Not by looking — not your friends, not a jeweler with a loupe. Distinguishing origins takes specialized laboratory instruments. Graded stones come with a report that states the origin plainly, and most lab-grown diamonds carry a microscopic laser inscription on the girdle. You will always know what your stone is; nobody else will unless you tell them.

So which should you choose?

  • Choose natural if the billion-year origin story is part of what the ring means to you, or if tradition and potential resale genuinely matter.
  • Choose lab-grown if you'd rather put the difference into the design, the setting or the size — or simply keep it.
  • Consider a colored stone — sapphires and rubies are tough enough for daily wear and carry a character all their own.

Neither choice is "better". We work with both — conflict-free and responsibly sourced — and you approve the exact stone before it's set. If you're unsure, say so at the consultation; talking it through is what the first conversation is for.

Questions

Good to know.

Are lab-grown diamonds real diamonds?

Yes. Lab-grown and natural diamonds are the same material — chemically, physically and optically identical — and they are graded by the same laboratories on the same four Cs. The difference is origin: one formed in the earth, the other grew in a lab.

Can a jeweler tell a lab-grown diamond from a natural one?

Not by eye, and not with a loupe. Telling them apart requires specialized laboratory instruments. Graded stones carry a report stating their origin, and most lab-grown stones are laser-inscribed on the girdle, so the provenance is never in doubt.

Do lab-grown diamonds hold their value?

Honestly: no diamond should be bought as an investment. Natural stones resell below retail; lab-grown resale prices are lower still and less predictable, since growing technology keeps improving. Buy the stone for the ring and the person, not the resale ledger.
