Platinum or gold? How to choose the metal for an engagement ring.

Color, wear, weight and price, compared plainly, so the metal you choose suits the way you live.

Published November 2026 · Azoir & Co., New York

The short answer

Choose the color first. If you want a warm ring, the answer is yellow or rose gold, and 14K or 18K is the next decision. If you want a white ring, the choice is between white gold, which is plated to look bright white, and platinum, which is white all the way through.

Platinum is denser, heavier and wears differently. Gold is lighter, comes in more colors and is easier to work. Both make excellent engagement rings. The right one depends on your taste, your hands and your budget.

Why the question matters right now

Metal prices have moved a lot this year. In mid-November, JCK noted that gold had dipped back below $4,000 an ounce (JCK). On November 17, 2026, Kitco showed gold near $3,857 an ounce (Kitco gold) and platinum near $1,430 an ounce (Kitco platinum).

So, ounce for ounce, platinum currently costs well under half as much as gold. That surprises many people, because platinum is often thought of as the luxury option. But a spot price is only the starting point. What you pay for a finished ring depends on how much metal it holds and how much work it takes, and that is where the two metals differ.

Weight and feel

Platinum is one of the densest metals used in jewelry. Pure platinum has a specific gravity of about 21.45, compared with about 19.3 for pure gold (CUNY Queensborough). Platinum jewelry is also usually close to pure, while 14K gold is only 58.5% gold, mixed with lighter metals. The result: the same ring made in platinum weighs noticeably more than it does in 14K.

Some people love that weight. It feels solid and substantial, and it is part of why platinum is prized. Others prefer a ring they forget they are wearing. If you can, try on a platinum band and a gold band of similar width before you decide. Our wedding band builder shows 14K, 18K and platinum side by side in 3D.

What the price gap really means

Because platinum is so dense, a ring uses more of it by weight. Platinum also melts at a far higher temperature than gold, which makes it harder to cast and slower to finish. Those two facts narrow the gap that spot prices suggest.

Gold, meanwhile, is sold by karat. 18K is 75% gold and 14K is 58.5%, so an 18K ring carries more gold than the same ring in 14K, and its price follows the gold market more closely. When gold moves by hundreds of dollars an ounce, as it has this fall, an 18K design feels it most.

The practical advice is simple. Do not choose by the spot price alone. Ask for your design quoted in two metals, and compare finished prices. Our guide to what a custom engagement ring really costs explains where the rest of the money goes.

White gold and rhodium plating

White gold is real gold, alloyed with whiter metals. On its own it has a soft, slightly warm tone, so it is usually plated with rhodium, a bright white metal, for that crisp finish. US rules treat plating as something a seller must disclose: under the FTC's Jewelry Guides it is unfair or deceptive not to disclose a surface layer of rhodium (16 CFR 23.7). Ask about it when you compare white rings.

Plating is not a flaw. It simply means a white gold ring will need a fresh coat from time to time to stay bright. Some white gold alloys also contain nickel, a common skin allergen, so if you react to costume jewelry, say so early. Our care & maintenance page covers everyday cleaning for both metals.

Matching the metal to the stone

The metal changes how a stone looks. A colorless diamond tends to look whitest in white prongs, which is why many rings pair a yellow gold band with a platinum or white gold head. Warm stones, such as champagne diamonds or orange sapphires, often glow in yellow or rose gold. Our guide to colored engagement rings goes into stone choice in more depth.

Think ahead to the wedding band, too. Bands sit against the engagement ring all day, and two different metals rubbing together wear at different rates. Many people match the metals, or choose the band's metal knowingly. The materials guide sets out every metal and stone we work with.

Questions

Good to know.

Is platinum better than gold for an engagement ring?

Neither is better for everyone. Platinum is naturally white, dense and wears by moving rather than losing metal, so it suits people who want a white ring that needs no plating. Gold costs less to work, comes in yellow, rose and white, and 14K is a durable everyday choice. Choose the color you love first, then the metal that gives you that color most easily.

Is platinum more expensive than gold right now?

Per ounce, no. In mid-November 2026, platinum traded near $1,430 an ounce and gold near $3,860. A platinum ring still is not automatically cheaper, because it uses more metal by weight and takes more work to cast and polish. Ask for a quote in both metals for the same design.

Does white gold turn yellow?

White gold is a gold alloy with a naturally pale, slightly warm tone. It is usually plated with rhodium for a bright white finish. As the plating wears, the warmer tone can show through, most often on the underside of the band. Replating restores the white.

Can I mix platinum and gold in one ring?

Yes. A common pairing is a yellow gold band with a white metal head, so a colorless diamond sits in white prongs while the band stays warm. Matching a wedding band later is simplest if you note which metal each part is made of.
